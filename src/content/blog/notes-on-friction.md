## Productive Friction

Friction is not always bad. A confirmation step before deleting a draft protects the user. A slower editorial cadence protects quality. A visible boundary between a claim and a source protects trust.

The bad kind of friction is accidental. It appears when a reader must manage windows, lose scroll position, decode vague categories, or resize text through browser-level controls because the page offers no reading preferences.

## Publishing Friction

Most blogs optimize the beginning of reading and neglect the middle. They make the index attractive, then leave the long article to behave like a static document. Thinker Scripts makes the middle of reading the primary design surface.

That is why the progress bar, time remaining ticker, and type controls are article-level tools rather than promotional features.

## A Useful Rule

If a reader has to stop thinking about the argument to operate the interface, the interface has become too loud.
